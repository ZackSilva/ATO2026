# Market data access and valuation evidence

Investigated October 20, 2026. The owner selected CardSight for automatic auction search and collection estimates and configured a private API key. The collector confirms each owned copy's exact provider identity once, then the app retrieves its auction evidence automatically. Manual completed-sale entry and upload are not product features. Free browsing of sold results alone does not establish automated API access.

## Automatic collector workflow

**Add card → Search cards** searches the catalog by player, year, set or number. **Use card details** retrieves an editable identity draft for review; neither action saves a physical copy, links it for pricing or supplies a value. Parallel labels remain suggestions, and a blank parallel does not establish a base card. Manual entry remains available.

Choose **Estimate Value** near the top of the editor to save the physical copy before opening pricing. When configured, the estimate flow automatically retrieves candidate matches; the collector must still review the exact issue, parallel and grading label and choose **Use this match** before automatic pricing starts. **Save card** saves without pricing and offers **Estimate Value** afterward. Existing cards expose the action prominently in their read-only details header. The pricing section on **Sold comparables** is also called **Estimate Value**.

Discover accepts a card query, time range and optional raw/graded/grader/grade filters. An explicit Search sales action calls the authenticated SlabTrak API; typing does not trigger provider requests. Card-detail links prefill that query. Prices, dates, sources and graphs come from the provider response, never user-uploaded CSVs or manual comparable forms. The historical sample evidence remains saved, but its entry controls and the uncommitted CSV feature are removed from the user flow.

[CardSight's pricing documentation](https://play.cardsight.ai/endpoints/pricing) identifies only auctions as completed transactions. Fixed-price/Buy It Now records are asking prices and must not enter the sold results. The title search can span several issues/variants, so the graph is search evidence rather than a personal portfolio estimate. Raw results retain unassessed physical condition; unfamiliar graded context remains unknown instead of silently becoming raw. Currency is USD according to the provider schema. Missing dates, future dates, nonpositive/invalid prices and contradictory currency are rejected.

On an owned card's **Sold comparables → Estimate Value** section, **Find exact card** proposes catalog identities. **Use this match** confirms the card and base/parallel identity for that physical copy, with an explicit grading label when several match. The server checks issue, year, number, release/subset, available sport/manufacturer references and parallel applicability. Missing or ambiguous identity stays unsupported; fuzzy Discover results never establish this association. Changes to identity, grading, raw condition or currency invalidate it. **Change match** and **Remove match** remain explicit actions.

[Certificate lookups](certificate-lookups.md) are a separate editable-entry aid. Configured, approved PSA access can propose identity/grade details for the collector to review and explicitly apply; it does not supply a market link or price. The live PSA check returned HTTP 403 for unapproved account access, with no successful live lookup demonstrated. Other graders retain manual entry and official verification links. The same **Estimate Value → Use this match** confirmation applies after certificate entry.

Confirmed copies use [exact-card pricing](https://play.cardsight.ai/endpoints/pricing) with auction-only, base/parallel and raw/grade-ID filters. Home, Reports and card graphs use the resulting matching observations automatically; unlinked cards keep their saved-evidence estimates. At least three matching sales within 180 days support a median and observed range. Linked copies show an unavailable or insufficient state when fresh evidence cannot support an estimate, with saved historical evidence available separately. Supported live prices are USD and either raw with unassessed condition or the exact graded label. Condition-specific raw pricing, other currencies, autograph/relic identities, separate variations and unverifiable variants remain unsupported. No grade multiplier, currency conversion or cost-to-value substitution is applied; unknown purchase costs remain unknown.

[Provider terms](https://cardsight.ai/terms), sections 3(b)–3(c), permit limited short-term application caching and require refresh/purge and deletion at termination. They do not authorize a standalone catalog or indefinite provider-sale/derived-estimate archive, and specify no numeric TTL. SlabTrak saves only confirmed reference IDs, their copy signature and confirmation time; provider sales and computed valuations never enter database tables, files or browser storage. Bounded server-memory caches expire pricing after 24 hours and catalog/grade references after 30 minutes, with periodic purge and shutdown clearing. Those durations are application choices, not provider-approved retention periods. **Refresh estimate** respects a five-minute cooldown. Existing saved historical evidence is preserved separately and is not overwritten or merged with provider responses.

Single-card pricing returns at most 500 recent rows; bulk pricing accepts up to 100 cards with an effective maximum of 50 rows per card. SlabTrak bounds automatic refresh work to three filter groups per read and reports possible truncation; it does not download complete sale histories. Matching, research and refresh share the advertised 750 monthly free calls and 4 requests/second. The local cache is process-specific, so separate workers or restarts can make additional calls. Invalid amounts/dates, contradictory currency, wrong identities/filters and non-auction records are rejected. Sources and accepted transaction prices remain provider-reported, not independently verified.

The authenticated, CSRF-protected catalog routes are `POST /api/market/catalog/search` (`query`, 4–200 characters) and `POST /api/market/catalog/card` (`card_id`). Search returns at most 20 drafts with a truncation flag and review note; selecting a result fetches its details. These queries use the bounded 30-minute server-memory catalog cache, and cache misses consume the same CardSight allowance as matching and pricing. Typing never sends catalog requests. Neither route writes collection records or confirms a market link.

Live provider access and fixture validation are distinct. The key remains in ignored server configuration. Local development trust uses an ignored override and a separate local Docker volume; certificates are never copied into application images or release source. Provider quotas, incomplete coverage, and upstream factual accuracy remain limits even after a successful query.

## Configure CardSight and inspect sales

Create a free [CardSight account](https://app.cardsight.ai), then add its API key to your local ignored `.env` as `CARDSIGHT_API_KEY`. Never commit the real key or use a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` setting for it. Recreate the API after changing configuration:

```sh
docker compose up -d --no-deps --force-recreate api
```

Open **Discover**, enter a card description and choose **Search sales**. Expand **Search filters** for raw/graded condition, grading company/grade and time range. Entering or editing text does not spend a search request until you submit. A card's **Find recent auction sales** link prefills the query for you. Results show reported completed auctions and source links; the graph describes only those returned prices, which may span multiple card issues or variants. Incomplete and non-auction observations are excluded. No manual sales entry or CSV import is part of this flow.

Each returned sale appears in an individual row with its listing-image preview, title, grade/condition, date, price and original source link. Hover or tap a point in the graph to inspect that sale and highlight its row. The selected-sale panel includes the photo and source link; arrow keys and Previous/Next controls also reach overlapping points. Missing or failed images show a fallback without hiding the sale. These are marketplace listing photos, not photos of your owned copy. Changing the graph period or inspecting a point makes no extra provider search request.

Saved-card graphs in card details and Sold comparables also expose individual sale details and source links when you hover, tap or use the arrow keys. Expand Sales for the dated records behind the graph; closing the dropdown leaves the graph and selected sale visible. Matching follows the API's exact card/currency/condition/grade rules. Saved sales retain optional original-listing image references and use the same previews as Discover. Older records without available images show a fallback; an owned-card photo is never substituted for a listing image. The full Recorded sales list is also collapsible.
